Brake Services

Brake service addresses the friction components and hydraulics that turn pedal input into controlled stopping. Pads, rotors, drums, calipers, hoses, lines, and fluid need different inspections and repair decisions.

D&S / SERVICE DEPT.03

Match the concern to the component

A squeal, pedal vibration, or pull can have several explanations. Pad thickness, rotor measurements, hydraulic condition, and movement at the calipers help establish what actually needs attention. ABS warning concerns belong in diagnostics as well as a mechanical brake review.

Symptoms to take seriously

  • A soft or sinking pedal, visible fluid loss, or sharply increased stopping distance.
  • Grinding, overheating at a wheel, or a strong pull during braking.
  • A parking brake that will not release or hold the vehicle as expected.

Start with safe operation

If braking is significantly impaired, stop safely and arrange professional help. The services below describe distinct repairs; a complete brake inspection can identify which combination is appropriate for your vehicle.

Services in this category

03.01

Brake Pad Replacement

Brake pads press against the rotors to slow the wheels. Replacement renews the friction material when inspection shows it is worn, damaged, or contaminated. Pad selection and the work required depend on the vehicle and the condition of the surrounding brake hardware.

Learn More 03.02

Brake Rotor Replacement

Brake rotor replacement renews the metal friction surface used by disc brake pads. A rotor may need replacement because of insufficient thickness, cracking, severe corrosion, or other damage established during inspection. Braking vibration by itself is not enough to identify the required repair.

Learn More 03.03

Brake Drum Replacement

Brake drums provide the rotating friction surface for brake shoes on vehicles equipped with drum brakes. Replacement is considered when the drum is oversized, damaged, cracked, or otherwise outside its service limits. Disc brake rotors and brake drums are different components and use different inspection criteria.

Learn More 03.04

Brake Wheel Cylinder Replacement

A brake wheel cylinder converts hydraulic pressure into movement of the shoes inside a drum brake. Replacement addresses a cylinder that leaks, sticks, or has damage that prevents reliable operation. It is distinct from a disc brake caliper and from the brake master cylinder.

Learn More 03.05

Brake Caliper Repair

Brake caliper repair addresses a serviceable problem within or around a disc brake caliper rather than automatically replacing the complete assembly. Whether repair is appropriate depends on the caliper design, damage, available service parts, and the ability to restore reliable operation.

Learn More 03.06

Brake Caliper Replacement

Brake caliper replacement renews the assembly that applies disc brake pads against the rotor. It may be needed when a caliper leaks, seizes, or has damage that makes repair unsuitable. The decision should follow inspection of the hydraulic circuit and the brake parts at the affected wheel.

Learn More 03.07

Electronic Parking Brake Repair

An electronic parking brake uses electrical controls and actuators to apply or release the parking brake. Repair may involve the switch, wiring, motorized mechanism, or related mechanical components. The design varies, so a warning message does not identify a single universal repair.

Learn More 03.08

Brake Hose Replacement

Brake hoses are flexible sections of the hydraulic circuit that allow suspension and steering movement while carrying brake pressure. Replacement addresses leakage, cracking, damage, or an internal restriction confirmed through inspection. A hose can develop an internal problem without obvious damage on its exterior.

Learn More 03.09

Brake Line Replacement

Brake lines are the rigid tubes that carry hydraulic pressure through the vehicle. Replacement addresses corrosion, physical damage, or confirmed leakage. Line routing, material, fittings, and support are safety-related details; a damaged section requires more than sealing its exterior or adding brake fluid.

Learn More 03.10

Brake Fluid Replacement

Brake fluid replacement renews fluid in the hydraulic brake system using the specification and procedure required for the vehicle. Many common brake fluids absorb moisture over time, which can affect boiling performance and contribute to internal corrosion. Replacement intervals and evaluation methods vary by manufacturer.

Learn More 03.11

Brake System Bleeding

Brake system bleeding removes trapped air from the hydraulic circuit. Air compresses under pressure and can change pedal feel or brake response. Bleeding is commonly required after a hydraulic component is opened, but the reason air entered the system should be understood rather than ignored.
