Engine Repair

Engine repair ranges from sealing an external oil leak to replacing damaged internal components or the complete engine. Choosing the right scope requires understanding whether the concern involves lubrication, compression, cooling, valve operation, mounting, or electronic inputs.

D&S / SERVICE DEPT.04

What is included

This category covers cylinder heads, head gaskets, valves, seals, piston rings, engine assemblies, oil pans, mounts, and engine sensors. Timing-drive work and cooling-system components have separate categories with closely related services.

Describe the concern before choosing a repair

  • Note smoke color, fluid loss, unusual noises, and when the symptoms appear.
  • Report previous overheating, low oil pressure, or a recent timing-system problem.
  • Bring any compression, leak, or diagnostic findings already available.

Evidence before major work

Oil consumption does not automatically mean piston rings are worn, and a rough engine does not automatically need replacement. The pages below explain specific repair paths. With overheating, a red oil-pressure warning, or severe knocking, stop safely and seek professional assistance rather than continuing to drive.

Services in this category

04.01

Head Gasket Replacement

A head gasket seals the joint between the engine block and cylinder head, separating combustion pressure, coolant, and oil passages. Replacement addresses a confirmed sealing failure. The work also needs to account for the condition of the mating surfaces and any underlying overheating or cooling problem.

Learn More 04.02

Cylinder Head Replacement

Cylinder head replacement renews the assembly above the engine cylinders when the existing head has damage that makes repair unsuitable. The head contains the combustion chamber area and valve-related components, with the exact arrangement depending on the engine. Replacement scope must match the specific engine design.

Learn More 04.03

Cylinder Head Resurfacing

Cylinder head resurfacing restores an acceptable sealing surface when measurement shows that machining is appropriate. It is generally part of a larger disassembled-engine repair, not a treatment performed through the outside of an assembled engine. The head must remain within its dimensional and finish requirements.

Learn More 04.04

Engine Valve Replacement

Engine valve replacement renews a damaged intake or exhaust valve that can no longer perform its sealing or movement function correctly. Valves control gas flow into and out of the cylinders. Replacement is an internal repair that should follow evidence of damage rather than a general misfire complaint alone.

Learn More 04.05

Valve Adjustment

Valve adjustment sets the specified clearance in engines with an adjustable valvetrain. Correct clearance allows the valves to close properly while accommodating temperature changes and component movement. Not every engine requires periodic adjustment; many use systems with different service requirements.

Learn More 04.06

Valve Stem Seal Replacement

Valve stem seals control the amount of oil that passes along valve stems into the intake or exhaust ports. Replacement addresses seals that have deteriorated or no longer control oil as intended. It is different from replacing the head gasket or repairing worn piston rings.

Learn More 04.07

Piston Ring Replacement

Piston ring replacement renews the rings that help seal combustion pressure and control oil on the cylinder walls. It requires substantial engine disassembly. The condition of the cylinders and pistons determines whether new rings are an appropriate repair or whether more extensive work is necessary.

Learn More 04.08

Engine Rebuild

An engine rebuild repairs an existing engine through disassembly, inspection, measurement, and renewal of the components needed to restore its intended operation. The scope is established by wear and damage rather than a universal list of parts. A rebuild is different from installing a replacement engine assembly.

Learn More 04.09

Engine Replacement

Engine replacement installs a compatible engine assembly when repair of the existing unit is not the preferred option. Compatibility involves more than physical fit: engine configuration, emissions equipment, electronics, and required supporting components must match the vehicle. The supplied assembly's contents can vary substantially.

Learn More 04.10

Valve Cover Gasket Replacement

The valve cover gasket seals the cover above the cylinder head and helps keep engine oil inside. Replacement addresses a confirmed leak at that joint or associated seals. Depending on the design, spark-plug tube seals and other cover-related components may also be relevant to the repair.

Learn More 04.11

Oil Pan Replacement

The oil pan forms the lower reservoir for engine oil. Replacement may be needed when the pan is cracked, dented in a damaging way, corroded, or has an unserviceable drain connection. Some oil leaks involve only a sealing joint, so inspection should determine whether the pan itself is defective.

Learn More 04.12

Engine Mount Replacement

Engine mounts support the engine and help manage its movement and vibration. Some use rubber and metal construction, while others include hydraulic or electronically controlled features. Replacement addresses a mount whose condition no longer provides the support or isolation required by the vehicle.

Learn More 04.13

Engine Oil Leak Diagnosis and Repair

Engine oil leak diagnosis identifies where oil escapes before deciding which seal, gasket, housing, or component needs repair. Oil can move across surfaces while driving, so a puddle's location does not reliably identify the failed part. The repair should follow the source, not just the most visible stain.

Learn More 04.14

Crankshaft Seal Replacement

Crankshaft seals retain engine oil where the crankshaft exits the engine at the front or rear. Replacement addresses a confirmed leak at the relevant seal. The front and rear locations have different access requirements, and nearby leaks can imitate either one.

Learn More 04.15

Camshaft Seal Replacement

Camshaft seals retain oil where a camshaft passes through a sealed opening in the cylinder head. Replacement is relevant on engines that use serviceable seals at those locations. A confirmed leak matters especially when oil can contaminate nearby timing-drive parts.

Learn More 04.16

Engine Sensor Replacement

Engine sensor replacement renews a sensor whose failure has been established through diagnosis. Engine sensors report conditions such as position, pressure, temperature, or airflow to the control system. Each sensor serves a different function, so this service is scoped to the identified component rather than a generic sensor package.

Diagnostics & Vehicle Inspections

Vehicle diagnostics turns a complaint into a focused investigation. Inspections and testing help distinguish a symptom from its cause, whether the concern is a warning light, unusual noise, braking change, or a vehicle you are considering buying.

Timing Belt & Timing Chain Services

Timing belts and chains coordinate the crankshaft and camshaft so the valves and pistons move in the intended relationship. The drive may include guides, tensioners, pulleys, and other engine-specific parts. Some engines also vary camshaft timing during operation.

Learn More 06

Cooling System Services

The cooling system manages engine temperature by circulating coolant and transferring heat through the radiator. Coolant, the pump, thermostat, hoses, radiator, and fan each have a different role. Correct service distinguishes a maintenance need from a loss of circulation, airflow, or sealing.
